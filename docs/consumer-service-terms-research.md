# Consumer Service Terms Research

**Reviewed November 15, 2026.** This note compares representative published policies and summarizes legal issues for attorney review. It is not legal advice, does not determine whether any competitor term is enforceable, and is not an approved customer contract.

## What the five company policies say

The two direct NYC locksmith examples illustrate different quote models. Sullivan Locksmith Services says its phone quotes are flat-rate before dispatch, after-hours charges are disclosed at quote time, and it does not do on-site upselling. Its terms also allow a possible trip fee after dispatch equal to its minimum on-site service fee, but do not state the amount or a specific notice time. The homepage and contact page separately advertise a free on-site estimate, so the public descriptions are not perfectly aligned. Sullivan states a 90-day workmanship warranty and says it will return at no additional charge to make a problem right. It names New York County courts, not arbitration. Its terms do not say what is due for unfinished work. [15] [16] [17]

SwiftLocksmith NYC says its website prices are estimates, final pricing follows an on-site assessment, and work requires the customer’s express approval of the quoted price. Its service page says listed prices include a service call and labor. The terms require two hours’ cancellation or rescheduling notice; emergency cancellation after dispatch may incur an unspecified service charge. The contact page shows emergency-rate windows that conflict internally. The company states a 90-day workmanship warranty, but the reviewed pages provide no partial-completion formula, detailed complaint escalation, or arbitration clause. [18] [19] [20]

Thumbtack’s terms govern its marketplace relationship and say the professional contracts directly with the customer. Its Guarantee uses a pre-work written scope, good-faith efforts to resolve the issue with the pro, and time-limited reporting; it is a conditional platform benefit, not a general locksmith payment or refund rule. Thumbtack’s own disputes use tiered arbitration, a small-claims exception, and an opt-out process. Those terms do not set a locksmith’s trip fee or the price due for partial work. [21] [22]

Handy’s platform terms describe a per-appointment Trust and Support Fee, cancellation fees, support contacts, and a limited Happiness Guarantee. Its non-California terms use AAA arbitration for covered Handy disputes, include a small-claims exception, and describe fee allocation. Handy’s terms and help materials conflict on one minimum-plan cancellation amount. Its booking platform is not a direct locksmith contract; its arbitration and guarantee provisions should not be treated as industry standards. [23] [24]

Taskrabbit’s terms and support policies use the Client–Tasker agreement and task chat to record scope and pricing. Its platform cancellation/no-show fee is generally tied to one hour of the Tasker’s rate plus platform fees or a partner minimum. Its guidance directs customers to raise quality or unfinished-work concerns through task chat and then support; its Taskprotect claims are limited and time-bound. U.S. terms provide AAA arbitration, preserve individual small-claims cases, and allocate some arbitration fees to the platform. These are platform-specific arrangements, not a direct-service locksmith fee or incomplete-work formula. [25] [26] [27] [28]

Across the two locksmith examples, a separately priced diagnostic fee is not stated. Their published cancellation charges differ, and neither reviewed terms page supplies a general rule for partial work or work that cannot be completed because of circumstances outside the company’s control. The samples therefore do not support adopting an automatic “half the job price” charge. A fee should reflect a real business practice, be defined against an objective base, be disclosed before authorization, and be reviewed by New York counsel.

## New York and federal issues that affect drafting

NYC’s current locksmith code requires a good-faith effort to identify the person requesting an opening and verify ownership or authority. It also requires specified records and the customer’s signature or mark on a prescribed authorization form for an opening. The code requires license-display steps before an opening. These are authorization and recordkeeping duties, not a customer service-call price schedule. The stated $50 in Administrative Code §20-300 is the locksmith license fee, not a customer charge. [1] [2]

Home-improvement rules may apply to some residential repair or installation work, depending on the work and statutory definitions and thresholds; they should not be assumed to govern every lockout. Where covered, New York and NYC rules require written contract disclosures, scope and price information, and cancellation notices. Progress payments must bear a reasonable relationship to the work, materials, or obligations incurred. Three-business-day cancellation disclosures and narrow emergency exceptions may apply. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule has separate coverage rules and exceptions for certain buyer-requested repairs, immediate bona fide emergencies, and wholly telephone/mail transactions. Counsel should determine which rules govern each service and how emergency requests are documented. [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

New York General Business Law §399-c defines a prohibited “mandatory arbitration clause” as one that requires arbitration before legal action and also makes the consumer-facing award final and not subject to court review. The Federal Arbitration Act generally enforces written arbitration agreements for transactions involving commerce, subject to its stated exceptions. Whether a particular clause is covered by §399-c, affected by federal preemption, or otherwise enforceable is a legal question for counsel—not something this comparison resolves. [8] [9]

Arbitration cannot truthfully be promised as entirely “outside any court system.” New York law permits court applications to compel or stay arbitration, confirm an award, and in defined circumstances modify or vacate an award. If AAA is selected, its consumer materials require a qualifying agreement and current rules; the published fee schedule limits consumer costs and assigns remaining administrative fees and arbitrator compensation to the business, subject to the rules. [10] [11] [12] [14]

Customers may also contact NYC DCWP. The agency accepts consumer complaints and says it uses mediation to resolve them. A company complaint channel can support dialogue, but the terms should not make internal escalation a waiver of statutory rights or an absolute prerequisite to contacting regulators or courts. [13]

The owner’s proposed requirement that the customer pay court costs “for both parties” should not be written as an unconditional obligation. CPLR §8101 generally entitles the party in whose favor judgment is entered to costs, subject to statutory exceptions and the court’s equitable discretion. General Obligations Law §5-327 can also make certain consumer-contract attorney-fee provisions reciprocal when a seller reserves recovery of attorney’s fees from a consumer. That statute concerns attorney’s fees, not every category of court cost, and counsel should review any proposed fee-shifting language. A cap limiting all customer recovery to amounts paid or agreed is also not approved or established here; its scope and enforceability require counsel review, and the terms should not waive rights that cannot legally be waived. [29] [30]

New York’s eavesdropping statute defines telephone wiretapping around recording by someone other than the sender or receiver without either party’s consent. That is not a complete answer for every call: calls crossing state lines, notice practices, vendor access, and storage should be reviewed. The owner confirms that phone calls are recorded for training and that the business retains its records for 18 months. Counsel should still review call-recording notice and consent practices, including any interstate calls and recording-provider access, before the policy is finalized. [31] [32]

NYC Administrative Code §20-301 requires locksmith opening records and prescribed authorization forms, and makes them available for inspection; 6 RCNY §2-141 specifies identifying, authority, location, timing, lock, and signature information for an opening. An official code-search result for §2-143 indicates an 18-month retention period for the forms, but the publisher’s direct section renderer did not expose §2-143’s text during this review. The owner separately confirms an 18-month business record-retention practice. The customer policy may describe that as the business’s practice, not as a blanket legal rule; verify the current code text and how it applies to each record category with NYC DCWP or counsel. The SHIELD Act also requires reasonable safeguards for covered private information; a policy statement should not substitute for the business’s actual security practices. [1] [33] [36]

## Confirmed business rules and remaining review

The owner confirms that the service-call amount varies by job type and the time of day the request is received; the owner has now excluded location as a pricing factor. There is no extra charge solely because an address is elsewhere in the Bronx. The applicable service-call amount must be communicated before dispatch. No service-call fee is due before dispatch; once a technician is assigned and dispatched or is on the way, the service-call amount is the minimum due, including after a customer cancellation or inability to complete for circumstances outside the business’s control. If the customer hires the business for the job, the service-call fee is credited toward the agreed job price.

For a partly completed or unfinished dispatched job caused by circumstances outside the business’s control, the owner confirmed the minimum due is the service-call amount plus any parts actually used. The draft does not introduce a “half the job” charge. Any additional labor or work charge must be disclosed as part of the agreed scope or separately approved before that work is performed. A specific service-call schedule was not supplied, so the amount must be stated to each customer before dispatch.

Customers may raise a concern by calling (646) 264-1901 and asking for a manager, or by writing to info@emergencylocksmithbronx.com. The business will follow up and try to resolve it as soon as possible; no fixed response deadline was supplied. The workmanship warranty lasts 10 days from job completion and excludes damage caused by the customer or a third party. After inspection, the business chooses whether re-work or a credit is appropriate and tells the customer during the inspection.

The owner confirms that phone calls are recorded for training and that all business records are retained for 18 months. No SMS is used, though a technician may leave voicemail. Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity are active on the customer-facing site for website analytics and improvement only, not advertising or remarketing. The owner reports that Clarity masks form inputs. The draft policy identifies these practices but does not make claims about provider-side retention beyond the business’s control.

The remaining legal approval concerns the proposed arbitration approach, the owner’s proposed allocation of both parties’ court costs to the customer, and the proposed cap on a customer’s recovery at amounts paid or agreed. The English and Spanish terms mark all three as counsel-review proposals, not operative contract terms. Counsel should also verify service-specific cancellation notices, the treatment of phone-call recording consent, and the precise application of locksmith record-retention rules before the pages are published as final.

## Separate implementation issue and remediation

The former English and Spanish form flow called a browser-side function that read `NEXT_PUBLIC_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID`. Next.js documents that `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variables referenced in client code are inlined into browser JavaScript during `next build`; a Cloudflare setting cannot protect a secret after it is compiled into public output. The site code has now been changed so all forms POST to the same-origin `/api/telegram-lead` endpoint, implemented as a Cloudflare Pages Function at `functions/api/telegram-lead.ts`. The Function validates the request origin, content type, body size, allowed form types, and field lengths; it sends the page path but not the query string to Telegram, uses server-only bindings named `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and `TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID`, avoids logging submitted information, and applies a best-effort per-isolate request limit. Cloudflare edge rate limiting is still recommended because an in-memory limit is not globally coordinated. [34] [35] [40]

Cloudflare requires the Pages `/functions` directory at the project root, outside the static output directory, and documents Wrangler as a supported deployment route. The repository workflow was updated from the deprecated `cloudflare/pages-action@v1` to Cloudflare’s current `cloudflare/wrangler-action@v4`, deploying `out/` from the repository root so the root Functions directory is available to Wrangler. No deployment was run; a future authorized preview must confirm the route is included. The old public-prefixed build variables must be removed, the new server-side secrets must be configured in the Cloudflare Pages project, and any bot token that was present in an already-deployed bundle should be rotated. The token was not retrieved or printed. A local generated `out/_next/static` scan found no token-shaped string, but that does not verify a production bundle or Cloudflare build settings. [41] [42] [43] [44] [45]

The owner confirms Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity are used only for website analytics and improvement, not advertising or remarketing, and that Clarity masks form inputs. Google requires website operators to disclose their use of Analytics; Microsoft documents its masking behavior. Telegram is a third-party processor for the lead notification flow, and its privacy policy is linked from the English and Spanish drafts. [37] [38] [39]
